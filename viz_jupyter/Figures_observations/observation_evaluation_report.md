# Observation Evaluation Report

This report summarizes the figures and metrics in `Figures_observations/pdf_figures`.
The annual map, structure, and spectra diagnostics compare the Samudra 2
1/4 degree rollout and the OM4 1/4 degree simulation against observation-based
products for 2022. ARGO-IAP monthly time-series diagnostics use the common
model/ARGO overlap from October 2014 through December 2022.

## Update Status

The updated evaluation has been run with ARGO-IAP temperature and salinity in
`nativegrid/argo-iap.zarr`. The stored PDFs and CSV metrics now include ARGO
temperature/salinity spatial-vertical structure, monthly OHC climatology,
temperature/salinity anomaly time series, ARGO-based temperature/salinity/OHC
maps, and surface geostrophic
KE temporal spectra against both raw daily DUACS and 5-day averaged DUACS.

The current local ARGO-IAP files cover January 1980 through December 2022. The
refreshed run uses the full 1980-2022 ARGO-IAP monthly climatology for
monthly climatology diagnostics and the common model/ARGO overlap for monthly
time-series comparisons. Deseasoned-anomaly figures are not generated by
default.

## Compared Models

- Samudra 2 (1/4 degree): emulator rollout from `predictions.zarr`
- OM4 (1/4 degree): reference ocean simulation from `OM4.zarr`

## Observation Datasets

- DUACS: satellite-altimetry product used for surface geostrophic velocity and
  geostrophic eddy kinetic energy (EKE).
- OISST: daily observed sea-surface temperature product used for regional
  surface-temperature spatial spectra.
- ARGO-IAP: monthly ocean heat-content / temperature / salinity product used
  for global monthly OHC comparisons and spatial-vertical structure down to
  2000 m.

## Preprocessing

DUACS and OISST are daily products. They were converted to the model's 5-day
rollout cadence by applying a centered 5-sample rolling mean, sampling on the
Samudra rollout dates, and relabeling to the exact rollout timestamps. The
resulting comparison products have 72 timestamps for 2022.

ARGO-IAP is monthly and was kept monthly. It is not forced onto the 5-day model
timeline, because that would add artificial high-frequency information.

Most diagnostics keep each observation product on its source native grid
(DUACS ~0.125°, OISST ~0.25°, ARGO-IAP ~0.5°). Spectra use each grid's own
`dx`/`dy`; OHC time series use each grid's own cell area and layer thickness.
The ARGO map and zonal-mean structure diagnostics explicitly interpolate model
annual means onto the ARGO latitude, longitude, and depth grid so pointwise
spatial/vertical structure can be compared against ARGO-IAP.

For model-native diagnostics, the finite ocean mask is taken from the OM4
reference grid and applied to both OM4 and Samudra 2. This avoids treating
finite Samudra predictions over land or invalid model cells as valid ocean
values.

## Evaluations Performed

### Surface geostrophic EKE vs DUACS

Files:

- `maps/eke_mean_vs_duacs_2022.pdf`
- `maps/eke_mean_lowpass_vs_duacs_2022.pdf`

DUACS geostrophic velocities are compared with model geostrophic velocities
derived from sea-surface height (`zos`). EKE is computed as
`0.5 * (u_g^2 + v_g^2)`. The equatorial band `|lat| <= 5 degrees` is masked.
The report includes both raw time-mean EKE and a Gaussian low-pass-filtered
version using `sigma = 0.5 degree`.

### Surface temperature isotropic spectra vs OISST

File:

- `spectrum/temperature_isotropic_spectra_vs_oisst.pdf`

Surface temperature anomalies are compared in four energetic regions: Gulf
Stream, Kuroshio, Agulhas, and Malvinas. The spectra are computed from regional
surface temperature anomalies after removing the time mean, applying linear
detrending and a Hann window. Spectral RMSE is computed on the spectral values
after interpolation to a common wavenumber axis.

### Surface geostrophic KE temporal spectra vs DUACS

File:

- `spectrum/ke_temporal_spectra_vs_duacs.pdf`

Regional temporal spectra are computed from surface geostrophic KE in the same
four regions. The model geostrophic velocities are derived from `zos`, while
DUACS provides observed geostrophic velocities. The figure includes raw daily
DUACS, 5-day averaged DUACS, and the model curves. Welch spectra are computed
along time, and RMSE is reported separately against raw daily DUACS and 5-day
averaged DUACS over overlapping frequency support.

### Monthly global OHC vs ARGO-IAP

Files:

- `timeseries/ohc_monthly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/ohc_anomaly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/ohc_monthly_climatology_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`

Global monthly OHC is compared in two depth layers:

- Surface: 0-700 m
- Intermediate: 700-2000 m

The 2000-7000 m layer is excluded because ARGO-IAP only extends to 2000 m in
this setup. OHC is computed from temperature, cell area, layer thickness,
seawater density, and heat capacity. Absolute monthly OHC RMSE, mean-removed
monthly OHC anomaly RMSE, and monthly OHC climatology RMSE are reported.

### Temperature/salinity time series vs ARGO-IAP

Files:

- `timeseries/temperature_monthly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/temperature_anomaly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/temperature_monthly_climatology_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/salinity_monthly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/salinity_anomaly_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`
- `timeseries/salinity_monthly_climatology_vs_argo_iap_201410_202212.pdf`

Global monthly layer-mean temperature and salinity are compared in the same
0-700 m and 700-2000 m layers as OHC. The metric is volume-weighted monthly
layer-mean RMSE against ARGO-IAP. Mean-removed anomaly, monthly climatology,
and absolute monthly variants are generated using the same definitions as the
OHC time-series diagnostics.

### Temperature/salinity/OHC maps vs ARGO-IAP

Files:

- `maps/temperature_layer_mean_maps_vs_argo_iap_2022.pdf`
- `maps/salinity_layer_mean_maps_vs_argo_iap_2022.pdf`
- `maps/ohc_layer_integrated_maps_vs_argo_iap_2022.pdf`

Annual-mean model temperature and salinity are interpolated to the ARGO-IAP
grid before layer maps are computed. Temperature and salinity maps show
layer-mean values; OHC maps show layer-integrated heat content per unit area in
`J m^-2`. Model maps are masked to the ARGO-IAP finite ocean mask before
plotting. Map color scales are based on the ARGO-IAP robust range, with model
values outside that range shown by colorbar extensions.

### Temperature/salinity/OHC structure vs ARGO-IAP

Files:

- `structure/argo_temperature_zonal_mean_bias_2022.pdf`
- `structure/argo_temperature_zonal_mean_2022.pdf`
- `structure/argo_salinity_zonal_mean_bias_2022.pdf`
- `structure/argo_salinity_zonal_mean_2022.pdf`
- `structure/argo_ohc_zonal_mean_bias_2022.pdf`
- `structure/argo_ohc_zonal_mean_2022.pdf`

Annual-mean model `thetao` and `so` are interpolated onto the ARGO-IAP latitude,
longitude, and depth grid over the common 5-2000 m range. Temperature, salinity,
and per-layer OHC are plotted as separate zonal-mean distribution figures for
ARGO-IAP, Samudra 2, and OM4, plus separate zonal-mean model-minus-ARGO bias
figures versus latitude and depth. Model fields are masked to the ARGO-IAP
finite ocean mask before the zonal means are computed. Temperature and salinity
metrics report area- and layer-thickness-weighted spatial/vertical RMSE; OHC
reports RMSE of per-layer heat-content bias in `J m^-2`.

## Main Results

Both Samudra 2 and OM4 substantially underestimate surface geostrophic EKE
relative to DUACS. DUACS global mean EKE is `1.52e-02 m2/s2`; Samudra 2 is
`3.77e-03`, and OM4 is `4.02e-03`. After low-pass filtering, DUACS is
`5.61e-03`, Samudra 2 is `1.14e-03`, and OM4 is `1.71e-03`. OM4 is closer to
DUACS in mean EKE, especially in the low-pass field.

For surface temperature isotropic spectra, OM4 has lower RMSE than Samudra 2 in
all four regions. The largest Samudra 2 disadvantage is in the Gulf Stream; the
Kuroshio difference is small.

For surface geostrophic KE temporal spectra, both models remain lower than
DUACS. OM4 has lower RMSE than Samudra 2 in all four regions against both raw
daily DUACS and 5-day averaged DUACS. The raw-daily comparison changes the
reference amplitudes most in Kuroshio and Agulhas; both raw and 5-day curves are
included in the refreshed figure.

For absolute monthly OHC, OM4 is much closer in the surface 0-700 m layer, while
Samudra 2 is slightly closer in the intermediate 700-2000 m layer. Both models
are biased high relative to ARGO-IAP in the intermediate layer.

For monthly OHC anomalies, OM4 is lower in both layers in the refreshed
2014-2022 comparison. Surface anomaly RMSE is `2.12e22 J` for Samudra 2 and
`1.52e22 J` for OM4. Intermediate anomaly RMSE is `8.39e21 J` for Samudra 2
and `6.77e21 J` for OM4.

For monthly OHC climatology, OM4 is closer in the surface 0-700 m layer
(`3.29e22 J` vs `1.03e23 J` for Samudra 2), while Samudra 2 is slightly closer
in the 700-2000 m layer (`6.42e23 J` vs `6.57e23 J` for OM4).

For monthly layer-mean temperature, Samudra 2 has lower absolute RMSE in the
surface layer (`0.303 degC` vs `0.383 degC` for OM4), while OM4 is slightly lower
in the 700-2000 m layer (`0.103 degC` vs `0.111 degC`). OM4 has lower
mean-removed temperature anomaly RMSE in both layers.

For monthly layer-mean salinity, OM4 is lower in the surface layer
(`0.130 psu` vs `0.137 psu` for Samudra 2), while Samudra 2 is slightly lower in
the 700-2000 m layer (`0.181 psu` vs `0.184 psu`). The salinity anomaly RMSE
split is similar: OM4 is lower at the surface and Samudra 2 is lower in the
intermediate layer.

For annual-mean temperature/salinity/OHC structure, Samudra 2 is slightly lower
than OM4 for all three refreshed spatial-vertical RMSEs. Temperature
spatial-vertical RMSE is `0.632 degC` for Samudra 2 and `0.639 degC` for OM4.
Salinity RMSE is `0.212 psu` for Samudra 2 and `0.215 psu` for OM4. Per-layer
OHC spatial-vertical RMSE is `1.26e8 J m^-2` for Samudra 2 and `1.28e8 J m^-2`
for OM4.

## Conclusion

The observation comparisons show a split result. OM4 is generally closer to the
observation products for absolute surface dynamical statistics, especially EKE
and spectral comparisons. Samudra 2 is more competitive for heat-content
variability and performs better for OHC anomalies. The dominant shared weakness
is that both Samudra 2 and OM4 are under-energetic relative to DUACS, indicating
that surface mesoscale variability is too weak in both products.
